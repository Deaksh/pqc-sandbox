1. Executive Summary

This memo presents the findings of a post-quantum cryptography (PQC) migration impact simulation conducted on Web API Server (ECDSA-P256 → ML-DSA-44). The simulation used the pqc-sandbox tool, running entirely on-premises with no data transmitted externally.

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) finalised three post-quantum cryptographic standards in August 2024: ML-KEM (FIPS 203), ML-DSA (FIPS 204), and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205). Cryptographically relevant quantum computers — capable of breaking current RSA and elliptic-curve encryption using Shor's algorithm — are projected by major intelligence agencies to arrive between 2030 and 2035. The "harvest now, decrypt later" (HNDL) threat means data encrypted today is at risk immediately.

The simulation identified a MEDIUM risk migration posture for Web API Server (ECDSA-P256 → ML-DSA-44). Specific cost, compatibility, and regulatory findings follow.

5. Business Risk Assessment

Operational risk: If migration is deferred and a cryptographically relevant quantum computer emerges, all historical data encrypted under current algorithms becomes recoverable by adversaries. Sensitive customer data, transaction records, and proprietary information face retroactive exposure.

Regulatory risk: Multiple Indian financial regulators (RBI, SEBI) and CERT-In have issued advisories on quantum preparedness. Non-compliance with future PQC mandates may result in enforcement action, audit findings, or loss of operating permissions.

Competitive risk: Enterprises that delay PQC migration until mandated will face compressed timelines, skills shortages, and higher vendor costs. Early movers will have completed the transition on their terms, with tested rollback plans.

Third-party / supply chain risk: Migration is only as strong as the weakest link. APIs, HSMs, certificate authorities, and SaaS vendors must also support PQC. Vendor qualification takes 6–18 months and should begin immediately.
